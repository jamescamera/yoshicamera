Every reading, by hour of day

Each cell is every reading at that hour, averaged. A ring marks an hour whose mean clears twice its own standard error.

What you are looking at. Every hourly reading of the last few months, re-indexed so that across is the hour of the day (00–23 UTC), down is the day of the week, and back is the week. Nothing is averaged away — each cell is one specific hour of one specific day. If the hour of the day matters, it shows as vertical banding that holds all the way back through the block. If the day of the week matters, it shows as horizontal banding. If neither does, it looks like confetti, and that is the honest answer rather than a failure to find one.

Why there is a control. A block of noise and a broken chart look identical, so one of the subjects is air temperature, which has a daily cycle nobody disputes. Switch to it and the banding is unmistakable and holds all the way back; switch away and you can see for yourself that the coins have nothing like it. Without something known to compare against, neither picture means very much.

The block is for spotting, not for reading. A turning, translucent solid is a bad instrument for precise values — it occludes, it foreshortens, it blends. Cut to a week and read it off the flat panel, or open the table.

It is a way of looking, not a signal. Nothing here is advice. A few months of one subject is a small sample, and testing twenty-four hours at once means the strongest-looking hour will look strong by chance more often than it first appears — which is exactly why the line above counts how many hours chance alone would have handed you.

Crypto rather than equities, for two reasons: it trades continuously, so the grain is unbroken where a market's nights and weekends would cut the block into stripes that say nothing about behaviour — and it is the only free price source that a page with no server can fetch at all, since the equities APIs either need a key or send no CORS header. Prices from CoinGecko, temperature from Open-Meteo, both free and without a key.
