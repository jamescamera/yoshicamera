# Shorter camera descriptions: drafts for review

These are the ten setup-sheet descriptions over 500 characters. Each draft keeps
what you need to use the camera (its controls, gestures and shutter behaviour)
and drops the explanation of how it works. The full versions would move into the
README, so nothing is lost. Nothing has been changed yet.

---

## Loaf (2,174 → 449 characters)

The last few seconds as a solid block, with time as its third axis. The face
towards you is now; the sides are one row of each moment laid edge to edge, so
anything that moved streaks back through it. Slices and Span set how much time;
Sweep cuts back through it, and in Pick the shutter takes the lit frame. Carve
leaves only what moved. Frozen stops new slices, so you can walk round a moment
that has passed. Drag to turn it.

## Turf (1,145 → 401)

Every colour is a faction and the colour wheel is the type chart: each beats the
ones just ahead of it and loses to the ones just behind, so the war never ends.
Hold is home advantage — past the middle the room wins, below it the war does.
Fury is how often they attack; Luck lets a losing attack win. Drag to carry the
colour under your finger into enemy ground. With Sound on, noise raises the fury.

## Pareidolia (1,066 → 454)

Pattern recognition enhancement, from the psychedelic replications archive:
finding faces in things that only nearly contain them. It needs no model,
because what it enhances is symmetry — it finds near-mirrors and folds the
picture towards its own reflection. Suggestion is how hard it pushes,
Selectivity how nearly perfect a mirror must be, and Level finds reflections in
water and glass. It knows there is a mirror there; it has no idea it is a face.

## Stitch (856 → 352)

A plate is taken — one frame, held — and the live picture is rebuilt from discs
punched out of it, each gone once cut. Hold still and the picture comes back
clean; move, and the past stops holding what the present asks for, so what goes
wrong is a record of what changed. Supply is how strictly it refuses to re-cut a
spent disc. Tap for a fresh plate.

## Wash (847 → 411)

Watercolour: every layer transparent, and the paper the only light — it is never
painted, only left. Tone goes down in a few flat washes. Wash edge strands
pigment at the rim as it dries, which is the medium's signature; Bleed makes
boundaries arrive crooked, Granulation settles pigment into the paper's tooth,
and Ink presses harder where the picture turns sharply. Drag to tilt the paper
and let the wet run.

## Impasto (799 → 374)

Paint with height as well as colour, lit at the end. It paints where the canvas
and the lens disagree most, so holding still lets the painting finish itself and
moving sends the brush after whatever changed. Strokes follow the direction the
picture changes least, and overlaps muddy like pigment. Drag to rake the light
across the surface; tap to scrape back to bare ground.

## Stain (755 → 353)

Gamut, spent on part of the picture. Nothing in here can detect a subject, so
your finger does it: tap, and the camera takes that to mean that colour, that
brightness, whatever is moving, or just there. The picked pixels get a palette
of exactly that many colours, each spent once; the rest is left, drained or
dimmed. The shutter develops at full size.

## Gamut (705 → 366)

A palette with exactly as many colours as there are pixels, every one spent,
none twice. Clump lets neighbouring pixels take neighbouring colours — up in the
dark, down for confetti. Local detail judges each pixel against its
surroundings, which is HDR pushed until it stops being reasonable. The shutter
develops a full-size photograph, which takes a second or two.

## Portal (672 → 328)

A rectangle held in the room by the gyroscope — turn away and it stays where you
left it, come alongside and it narrows to an edge. Tap to move it. It knows
which way you face, not where you stand, so walking up to it will not make it
bigger. If it creeps against the room as you turn, nudge Match the lens until it
holds still.

## One Bit (579 → 283)

Two colours and nothing else; the greys you can see are not there. The error
from forcing each pixel is handed on to the ones not yet decided. Atkinson
snaps, Floyd–Steinberg holds more shadow, Threshold does not dither at all.
Even out the light keeps a half-shadowed face readable.

## Passes (572 → 323)

One frame shown as the layers a machine would work from: Surface reads
brightness as height, Flat divides out the broad shading to leave something
nearer a thing's own colour, and Marks are real corner detection. There is no
depth pass — depth cannot be had from one still frame without a model, and there
are none in here.
